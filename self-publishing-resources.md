# Self-Publishing Resources

**Last checked:** June 16, 2026

A practical resource list for authors who want to publish independently. This list favors official platform pages, current help centers, trusted industry sources, and tools that are useful for fiction and nonfiction authors.

> **Note:** Links change over time. Before paying for any service, check recent reviews, confirm pricing, read the terms, and make sure you keep control of your rights.

---

## Quick Start

If you are brand new, start here:

1. Finish and revise the manuscript.
2. Get outside feedback from critique partners, beta readers, or an editor.
3. Choose your publishing path:
   - **Amazon-only:** easier, especially with Kindle Unlimited.
   - **Wide distribution:** more stores, more control, slower growth for some genres.
   - **Print-focused:** more attention to ISBNs, trim size, print files, bookstore/library access.
4. Format the book for ebook and print.
5. Create a professional cover.
6. Prepare metadata: title, subtitle, series name, categories, keywords, description, ISBN, price.
7. Upload to your chosen platforms.
8. Order and review a print proof.
9. Build a launch plan.
10. Keep marketing, testing, and improving after release.

### Common Red Flags

Be careful if a company:

- Calls you out of nowhere and says your book has been “selected.”
- Guarantees bestseller status, film deals, bookstore placement, or media coverage.
- Pressures you to pay quickly.
- Claims you must buy expensive marketing packages to stay published.
- Hides fees until late in the process.
- Controls your ISBN, files, rights, or account access.
- Refuses to give clear answers about royalties, rights, distribution, or termination.
- Uses fake-looking testimonials, AI staff photos, or vague company history.

## Genre-Specific Notes

### Fiction

Focus heavily on:

- Genre fit.
- Cover expectations.
- Series strategy.
- Pricing strategy.
- Read-through.
- Mailing list growth.
- ARC/review team.
- Comparable authors.

### Nonfiction

Focus heavily on:

- Authority and credentials.
- Clear promise to the reader.
- Searchable subtitle.
- Problem/solution positioning.
- Speaking, consulting, courses, or services.
- Direct sales and lead generation.

### Children’s Books

Focus heavily on:

- Illustration rights and contracts.
- Print quality.
- Read-aloud experience.
- School, library, and parent markets.
- SCBWI and children’s publishing norms.

### Comics and Graphic Novels

Focus heavily on:

- Page size and color printing cost.
- Lettering quality.
- Creator agreements.
- Kickstarter/direct sales.
- Conventions and fan communities.

## Launch Checklist

Use this as a basic pre-launch checklist:

### Manuscript

- [ ] Final manuscript complete.
- [ ] Developmental edit or critique complete.
- [ ] Copyedit complete.
- [ ] Proofread complete.
- [ ] Front matter complete.
- [ ] Back matter complete.
- [ ] Copyright page complete.

### Production

- [ ] Ebook file exported and checked.
- [ ] Paperback interior exported and checked.
- [ ] Hardcover file prepared, if applicable.
- [ ] Cover files created for each format.
- [ ] Print proof ordered.
- [ ] Print proof reviewed.
- [ ] Final files uploaded.

### Metadata

- [ ] Title and subtitle finalized.
- [ ] Series title and volume number set.
- [ ] Author name/publisher name consistent.
- [ ] Book description polished.
- [ ] Keywords researched.
- [ ] Categories selected.
- [ ] BISAC/Thema codes selected, if needed.
- [ ] ISBNs assigned, if using your own.
- [ ] Price selected for each market.
- [ ] Publication date selected.

### Marketing

- [ ] Author website or landing page live.
- [ ] Email signup form live.
- [ ] Reader magnet prepared, if using one.
- [ ] ARC list prepared.
- [ ] Review copy delivery set up.
- [ ] Launch emails drafted.
- [ ] Social posts drafted.
- [ ] Ad images prepared, if running ads.
- [ ] BookBub/Goodreads/Amazon author profiles claimed.
- [ ] Direct buy links gathered after publication.

## Final Notes

Self-publishing is not just uploading a book. It is publishing, production, metadata, distribution, marketing, reader relationship, and business management.

The simplest beginner path is usually:

- Publish ebook and paperback through **KDP**.
- Add **IngramSpark** later for broader print distribution.
- Use **Draft2Digital** when you are ready to go wide.
- Build an email list early.
- Avoid expensive publishing packages that promise outcomes they cannot control.
- Keep your rights, files, accounts, and passwords under your control.

Happy publishing!
